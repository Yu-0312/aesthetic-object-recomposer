# Composition System

Choose the simplest structure that makes the supplied objects feel intentional.

## Decision table

| Composition | Best for | Placement logic | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagonal | Two objects, product + prop, drink + dessert | Place the hero on one third and the support across the opposite diagonal | Objects touching frame corners; equal visual weight |
| Triangle | Three objects or one tall + two low objects | Put the tallest or strongest object at the apex; form a stable broad base | Perfect symmetry; identical spacing |
| S-curve | Multi-item table spread, tea service, food sequence | Lead the eye through foreground, middle, and background with a soft S path | Excess props; sharp zigzags |
| L-shape | Book + camera, plate + utensils, desk scenes | Use one long horizontal anchor and one vertical or tall secondary anchor | Rigid right angles and empty dead corners |
| Table corner | Café tables, multiple dishes, intimate gatherings | Let a table edge enter diagonally; cluster objects near but not on the corner | Centering everything; cutting through the hero |
| Centered | One iconic object, hero product, single drink | Place the hero near center with subtle asymmetry in props and shadows | Flat catalog lighting; excessive empty margins |
| Negative space | One or two elegant objects, copy-safe social post | Keep objects on a lower or side third and reserve 35–55% calm space | Random tiny props in the empty field |
| Rule of thirds | General-purpose food, decor, camera, product | Put the hero at a thirds intersection; use background geometry to support it | Placing every object on a grid point |

## Selection rules

- One hero only → centered, thirds, or negative space.
- Two objects → diagonal or L-shape.
- Three objects → triangle by default.
- Four or more objects → S-curve or table-corner grouping.
- A tall glass or vase plus low plate → triangle.
- Camera plus open book → L-shape or thirds.
- Existing strong table edge → table-corner or diagonal.
- Large quiet window or wall → negative space.

## Spatial recipe

1. Place the hero first.
2. Establish one directional line using a table edge, book spine, utensil, chair, shadow, or window frame.
3. Add one counterweight opposite the hero.
4. Create height rhythm: low, medium, high or high, low, medium.
5. Check that the eye returns to the hero rather than exiting the frame.
6. Remove any prop that does not strengthen the composition or story.
